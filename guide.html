5 Signs Your Home Network Is Limiting Your Internet Plan

Your internet provider delivers speed to one box: the modem. What happens after that box decides what you actually get in each room. If any of these sound familiar, the problem is probably your home network, not your plan.

1 Speed tests look great next to the router, and bad everywhere else

Wi-Fi signal drops quickly through walls, floors, and appliances. A single router in one corner of the house can’t cover a two-story home evenly.

Try this: Run a speed test on your phone standing next to the router, then in the bedroom, the office, and the farthest room. If the far rooms get less than half of what you get by the router, coverage is the bottleneck.

2 Video calls freeze when someone else starts streaming

Your plan may have plenty of bandwidth, but an overloaded router or a crowded Wi-Fi channel can’t handle several busy devices at once. Calls are usually the first thing to break.

Try this: Notice whether problems line up with busy times, like evenings or when the kids are gaming. If they do, the network is struggling under load.

3 Cameras and smart devices keep going offline

Cameras, doorbells, and smart plugs are often placed at the edge of your coverage: on the porch, in the garage, at the back of the house. They also tend to use older, slower Wi-Fi.

Try this: Check your camera or smart-home app for “offline” or “reconnecting” history. Frequent drops mean weak signal or devices competing with everything else on one network.

4 Your router is the box the ISP gave you, and it lives in a cabinet

ISP combo boxes are built to be cheap and easy to support, not to cover your whole home. Putting one in a basement, closet, or media cabinet makes it worse. Older routers may also not support current Wi-Fi standards that newer phones and laptops can use.

Try this: Look at where your router sits. Is it out in the open, central, and up off the floor? Check the label for its model and look up when it came out. If it’s more than about five years old, it’s worth a look.

5 You don’t know what’s connected to your network

The average home now has dozens of connected devices. If you can’t see them, you can’t tell what’s hogging bandwidth, what needs a security update, or whether something doesn’t belong.

Try this: Open your router’s app or admin page and look at the device list. Count the entries marked “unknown” or with names you don’t recognize. If you can’t log in at all, that’s worth fixing too.
